Read [current-context-what-next.md](current-context-what-next.md) first. Use native harness tools for editing, browser interaction, and review. This is the local web companion to the coursework, with an independent Git repository.

- Treat the upstream `RawData/`, `PreparedData/`, submitted proposal, Word logs, and ArcGIS project as source inputs. Keep dashboard code, generated assets, and browser data snapshots in this folder. Follow the parent project’s process log and handoff rules.
- Keep browser runtime dependency-free (Google Fonts only). `index.html`, `styles.css`, `atlas.css`, `app.mjs`, `atlas.mjs`, `map.mjs` and `iso.mjs` render `data/`. `tools/export-inputs.py` uses GDAL only for offline geodatabase export and gridding (`PEAT_PROJECT_DIR` overrides the project path). `tools/model.mjs` and `tools/build-data.mjs` implement the models; `tools/stats.mjs` writes the significance and sensitivity checks to `data/stats.json`.
- Slide 1 (`#atlas`) leads with fire change. It opens on Jul–Oct 2026 with the big number = detections 2026 vs 2019 (same VIIRS sensor), a "where fire changed" diverging-bar list of child regions, and a layer bar: Fire 2019 / 2026 (both can be on; 2019 spikes are indigo and drawn beside the ember 2026 spikes), Smoke 2019 / 2026 (one year at a time) and Change 2019 → 2026. The default is Fire 2026 + Change. The panel's year buttons set the panel numbers and switch the layers to that year. The change layer colours regencies by (2026 + 5) / (2019 + 5): ×2+ dark red, ×1.25–2 red, ×0.8–1.25 grey, ×0.5–0.8 light green, ≤ ×0.5 green; regencies with < 20 detections in both years stay blank. It is the full-screen opening map: markup at the top of `index.html`, `atlas.css` (scoped to `.atlas`, ids and classes prefixed `at-`), `atlas.mjs`. It draws `data/atlas.json` (0.05° Indonesia land grid, base64 typed arrays aligned to WorldPop) and `data/atlas-bounds.json` (simplified HDX province/regency outlines with exact point-in-polygon fire counts); rebuild both with `python tools/export-atlas.py`. "Heavy fire" = 10+ detections per 5 km cell (`FIRE_MIN`); cells below that get no spike; spike height is a square root up to 1,600 detections/100 km² (`DMAX`). 2026 fire combines the Sumatra + Kalimantan file the story uses (so Riau/Kalteng stay 3,158 / 164,333) with FIRMS archive request 855893 for every other province (`FIRE` in `tools/export-atlas.py`). The smoke layer is `data/atlas-smoke.png`: Sept 2019 WashU PM₂.₅ over 92–142° E, 11° S–8° N in Web Mercator, classed at 15 / 35.5 / 55.5 / 150.5 / 250.5 µg/m³, with neighbour-city values in `atlas.smoke.cities`. For 2026 the map uses the October peak only: CAMS monthly mean on a 0.5° grid for all Indonesian land, from `data/cams-indonesia.json` (`python tools/cams-indonesia.py`: copies the story's `regional.json` values inside 95–120° E, 7° S–7° N and fetches the rest from Open-Meteo the same way). It is drawn as blocks. Open-Meteo has no CAMS for 2019, so the two seasons' smoke comes from different sources. The bottom strip is a chain of 100 figures holding hands (50 on phones) for Sept 2019 and the chosen 2026 month, coloured by smoke class (`SMOKE_BREAKS`) with largest-remainder rounding, plus the share and number of people above 55 µg/m³. Smoke shares of people are reported only where at least 90% of a region's people are covered, so Indonesia 2026 shows "—". City labels and the border sentence use the story's city values. Plain wheel scrolling passes to the deck; zoom needs pinch, Ctrl/⌘ + wheel, double-click or the buttons. Region and year live in `?r=&y=` (the deck owns `#slide`). It follows the deck's language through `html[lang]`; its strings are in the `T` table in `atlas.mjs`. Priority zones on the map come from `scenario-<year>-count-100.json` for the 13 study provinces.
- Slide `#budget` (`plan.mjs`) is the recommendation: an interactive Pareto chart of the wind-aware MILP plans from `data/plan.json` (written by the coursework `Paper/analysis.py` part P), a map of the funded regencies, a ranked list with the city each protects, and a button that sends the chosen plan to the opening map (`plan:show` event in `atlas.mjs`).
- The page is a scroll-snap deck (interaction copied from rzrizaldy.github.io/hw02-rag-companion): `.slide` sections, side dots, chapter progress, keyboard paging. The story always shows the count / 100 km setting.

## Illustration

`assets/peat-to-lungs.png` is an illustrative scene generated using the built-in ImageGen tool. The final prompt follows.

> Wide landscape editorial isometric illustration for a rigorous public-interest GIS data story on Indonesian peat fires. One continuous miniature land cutaway on a quiet muted mist-blue background (#dce9e9): left section drained tropical peatland, deep layered dark brown organic soil and a narrow drainage canal, a small glowing low ground fire releasing a thin pale grey smoke plume. Right section a modest cluster of Indonesian roofed homes and green trees at ground level in the direction of the drifting plume. Show the dark peat layer clearly as a geological cross section; communicate the mechanism of smoke reaching settlements. Subtle warm burnt orange for the embers, blue-green for the vegetation, soft daylight, matte tactile textures, confident and mature scientific-editorial art direction. All narrative elements contained within a single floating isometric landscape tile with clear negative space at the edges. No people required. No lettering, no arrows, no labels, no text, no UI, no lettering-like marks, no brand, no fireball. Similar proportions to a 3:2 magazine vignette, central composition.
